FINAL REPORT: DOSE-RESPONSE EFFICACY OF TEST COMPOUND IN A
THERAPEUTIC MODEL OF DIABETES-INDUCED NEUROPATHIC PAIN
Project Title: DIP-LPI-1
Project: To evaluate dose-response therapeutic efficacy of Lpath,
Inc. test compounds in a rat model of diabetes-induced
peripheral neuropathic pain. This study was carried out in
male Sprague-Dawley rats according to Washington
Biotechnology, Inc. Protocol 1090.
Sponsor: Rosalia Matteo, Ph.D.
Senior Scientist
Lpath, Inc.
4025 Sorrento Valley Blvd
San Diego, CA 91913
Summary of Results: Twice weekly intravenous injection with 30 mg/kg LT1015
(nonspecific IgG2b Control), starting five weeks after disease
induction, had no effect on the peripheral neuropathic pain
associated with Streptozocin-induced diabetes.
Twice weekly intravenous injection with 504B3 (IgG2b
murine anti-LPA antibody), starting five weeks after disease
induction, had a transient effect on the peripheral
neuropathic pain associated with Streptozocin-induced
diabetes at the highest dose (30 mg/kg).
Twice weekly intravenous injection with 30 mg/kg LT3114
(Human anti-LPA antibody), starting five weeks after disease
induction, was efficacious in reducing the sensitivity of
diabetic rats to both thermal and mechanical stimulation.

1. November 15, 2012: Received via Federal Express on coolants from Lpath, Inc.
and stored at 4-8OC:
• 24 cryovials LT1015, lot HR121332, 12.74 mg/ml
• 34 cryovials 504B3, lot HR111266, 11.98 mg/ml
• 34 cryovials LT3114, lot LP101202, 9.43 mg/ml
2. November 20, 2012: 70 Sprague-Dawley rats (Harlan Sprague-Dawley, Inc., PO
#659023, R #3185, 170-190g, male) were received,
individually examined, and housed in fourteen cages of five
rats each. Each animal was in apparent good health: no
signs of clinical disease or distress. The animals were
placed in quarantine under 12 hr light/dark conditions with
daily inspections, provided with ad libitum access to food
and water.
3. November 26, 2012: The animals were individually examined and observed to be
free of clinical signs of disease or distress. The animals
were released to routine maintenance. No deaths were
recorded during the quarantine period.
4. November 27, 2012: The rats were ear notched (SOP 800) for identification
purposes and weighed. Eight animals (rats #1-7, 11) were
assigned to Group 1 (non-diseased) based upon average
weight.
5. November 28, 2012: The body weight and baseline response to thermal
stimulation using the Hargreaves apparatus (IITC, model
336, SN 70108) was recorded for all rats (RESULTS). The
rats were acclimated (15 minutes) to the apparatus which
consisted of six separated Plexiglas chambers on a glass
table. A mobile radiant heat source located under the table
was focused under the desired hind paw. Paw withdrawal
latency (seconds) was recorded three to five times for each
hind paw and the average taken as the value for that limb. A
timer automatically activated with the light source and the
latency was defined as the time required for an abrupt
withdrawal of the hind limb. The light source was calibrate to
yield a normal latency of approximately 10-12 seconds; to
prevent tissue damage, the light source automatically cut off
at 20 seconds.

6. November 29, 2012: The body weight and baseline response to mechanical
stimulation using von Frey fibers was recorded for all rats
(RESULTS). Each rat was placed in a chamber with a metal
wire mesh floor and was acclimated for 15 minutes. Starting
with a probe diameter of 1.65 (0.008 grams of pressure) to a
diameter of 6.65 mm (300 grams of pressure), the probes
were systematically applied to the plantar surface of the hind
paw until paw withdrawal occurred.
7. December 4-6, 2012: The rat weights were recorded daily.
8. December 6, 2012: 0.01M citrate buffer was prepared by dissolving 0.384 g citric
acid (Fisher Scientific, Cat. A940-500, lot 025173) in 199.86
ml de-ionized water. The pH was adjusted to 4.5 with 3N
NaOH.
9. December 7, 2012: 1.22 g streptozocin (STZ, Sigma Chemical Co., Cat. S0130,
lot SLBB7526V) was dissolved in 110.909 ml 0.01M citrate
buffer to prepare a 11 mg/ml solution which was stored at 4-
8OC when not in use.
DAY 0 The rat weights were recorded (RESULTS). The rats in
Group1 were injected intravenously (IV, SOP 1650) with 5
ml/kg 0.01M citrate buffer. The remaining rats were injected
IV with 5 ml/kg of STZ solution (55 mg/kg). All rats were
individually housed on elevated flooring with daily cage
changes.
10. December 11, 2012: Rat #10 was found dead.
11. December 14, 2012: The rats were anesthetized (SOP 1810), bled (SOP 1660)
DAY 7 via retro-orbital sinus, and blood glucose recorded
(RESULTS, One Touch Ultramini, lot 3364714, exp.
0482014). All STZ-injected rats had blood glucose values in
excess of 300 mg/dl and were defined as diabetic per the
parameters of the protocol.
12. December 24, 2012: Rat #39 was found dead.
13. January 2, 2013: The response to thermal stimulation using the Hargreaves
DAY 26 apparatus was recorded for all rats (RESULTS).
14. January 3, 2013: The response to mechanical stimulation using von Frey
DAY 27 fibers was recorded for all rats (RESULTS). Since the
diabetic rats were allodynic, dosing was not initiated.

Discussion and Conclusions:
Intravenous injection with 55 mg/kg streptozocin resulted in diabetes in rats as defined
by a blood glucose value in excess of 300 mg/dl seven days later. Approximately one
month after disease induction, the diabetic rats exhibited peripheral neuropathy as
indicated by increased sensitivity to thermal (decreased response time) and mechanical
(decrease fiber diameter/pressure) stimulation. The rats were sorted into treatment
groups based upon the response to thermal stimulation in the left hind paw. Twice
weekly intravenous injection with vehicle or antibodies was initiated five weeks after
disease initiation and carried out for four weeks.
Twice weekly injection with 50 mg/kg LT1015 (Group 3) had no effect on diseased rat
weight gain. This treatment regimen had no ameliorative effect on the diabetic
peripheral neuropathy (DPN). On DAY 59, the rats in Group 3 were injected
subcutaneously with 30 mg/kg gabapentin thirty minutes prior to evaluation of the
peripheral response to thermal and mechanical stimulation. This treatment resulted in a
significant increase in the paw pressure required to elicit a response to the mechanical
stimulation, but had no effect on the Hargreaves test, relative to the saline-treated
(Group 2) diseased animals.
Twice weekly injection with 504B3 affected the diabetic rat weight at the highest dose
(30 mg/kg, Group 5). A significant improvement of DPN was observed at this dose
starting at week 7.
The LT3114 treatment regimen (Group 6) had no effect on diabetic rat weight. After
the first week (DAY 38) of this treatment regimen, the rats exhibited significantly
increased pain latency in response to thermal stimulation. This treatment regimen also
