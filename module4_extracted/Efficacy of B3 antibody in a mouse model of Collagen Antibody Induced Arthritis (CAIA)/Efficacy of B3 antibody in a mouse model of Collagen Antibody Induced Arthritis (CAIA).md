Title: Efficacy of B3 antibody in a mouse model of Collagen Antibody Induced Page 4 of 16
Arthritis (CAIA)
1 SUMMARY
This efficacy study in the collagen antibody-induced arthritis (CAIA) model was conducted
at the Department of Physiology and Pharmacology and Neuroscience, at the Karolinska
Institute, (Stockholm, Sweden). The purpose of the study was to demonstrate efficacy of the
murine anti-lysophosphatidic acid (LPA) antibody (B3) to ameliorate CAIA-mediated
mechanical and thermal hypersensitivity in the CAIA model.
Arthritis was induced in male CBA mice by i.v. injection of a cocktail of anti-collagen
antibodies on Day 0 followed by i.p. injection of 25 µg of lipopolysaccharide (LPS) on
Day 5. Studies were conducted either in the prophylactic mode (dosing started 8 days before
induction of arthritis and continuing to day 14) or in treatment mode (dosing started on
Day 12 and continuing until end of study). In both cases antibodies (control, LT1015,
murine, B3 or humanized, LT3114) were administered subcutaneously twice weekly. In the
prophylactic study, treatment with either B3 or LT3114 (10mg/kg; 6 mice/group) prevented
CAIA induced mechanical and thermal (cold sensitivity) but had no effect on the clinical
arthritis score. When dosing was delayed until day 12, at the height of the inflammatory
response, B3 (10 mg/kg; 6 mice/group) reversed CAIA-induced mechanical hypersensitivity
beyond Day 39 (time at which all inflammation is resolved). However, B3 dosed at 40 mg/kg
had no effect on mechanical allodynia and was similar overall to animals treated with control
antibody. In this study B3 was also effective at reversing heat hypersensitivity but was only
effective against cold allodynia in the inflammatory phase of the study.
In summary, LT3114 and B3 have shown effectiveness in a model of hypersensitivity as a
result of collagen antibody induced arthritis in both prophylactic and therapeutic dosing
modes.

2 INTRODUCTION
The bioactive lipid lysophosphatidic acid (LPA) is a key extracellular signaling molecule in
neuropathic pain. LPA and its receptors are responsible for long-lasting mechanical allodynia
and thermal hyperalgesia as well as demyelination and upregulation of pain-related protein
expression patterns in a variety of animal models. Evidence supports the hypothesis that
antagonism of the LPA signaling pathways would benefit the treatment of neuropathic pain.
We have developed the first monoclonal antibody targeting LPA (anti-LPA Ab). In
preliminary studies, Lpath’s anti-LPA mAb has demonstrated efficacy in the Seltzer model
of neuropathic pain where the literature has demonstrated a role for LPA in the disorder, we
have also demonstrated efficacy of the anti-LPA antibody in ameliorating neuropathic pain in
animals models of type 1 and 2 diabetes. We next wanted to expand the efficacy studies to
explore the effect of the murine antibody, B3, hypersensitivity in a model of chronic arthritis
pain. The pain that accompanies chronic rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is characterized as having
both inflammatory and non-inflammatory components and this non-inflammatory component
of pain shares many aspects of the neuropathic pain seen in other disease states. This pain
can be reproduced in mouse models of RA including the K/BxN serum transfer model and
the collagen antibody induced arthritis (CAIA) model. This study was performed in the
laboratory of Dr Camilla Svensson at Karolinska Institute. Dr. Svensson has recently
characterized the collagen antibody-induced arthritis (CAIA) model using pain readouts
beyond the inflammatory phase of the disease and established that the antibody-induced
arthritis generates persistent pain-like behavior that outlasts joint inflammation by weeks, a
characteristic that mimics the human disease. Thus, these studies were undertaken to
determine the efficacy of anti-LPA antibody treatment in relieving chronic non-inflammatory
pain in the CAIA model.

3.2 Study Design
3.2.1 Prophylatic treatment
Male CBA mice were habituated to the vivarium for 7 days after arrival. At the start of the
study animals (6 animals/group) were assessed for baseline allodynia (Day -9) followed the
next day (Day -8) by subcutaneous injection of LT3114, B3 or control antibody LT1015 at
10 mg/kg. Dosing continued twice a week until the end of the study. Arthritis was induced by
i.v. injection of a cocktail of anti-collagen antibodies on Day 0 followed by i.p. injection of
25 µg of lipopolysaccharide (LPS) on Day 5. Allodynia and arthritis clinical scores were
measured at the times detailed below.
3.2.1.1 Experimental outline (prophylactic treatment)
Day -9: Baseline tactile allodynia assessed
Day -8: Subcutaneous injection of LT3114, B3 or LT1015. Antibodies
injected 2 times / week after this point to the end of the study.
Day -7: Baseline tactile allodynia assessed
Day -4: Baseline tactile allodynia assessed
Day 0: Intravenous injection of collagen antibody cocktail (1.5 mg/mouse)
Day 5: Intraperitoneal injection of 25 μg LPS to boost arthritis
Day 7: Tactile allodynia and clinical score assessed
Day 10: Tactile allodynia and clinical score assessed
Day 13: Tactile allodynia and clinical score assessed
Day 14: Cold hypersensitivity assessed
Day 15: Tactile allodynia and clinical score assessed End of Experiment.
3.2.1.2 Experimental outline (Therapeutic intervention)
Male CBA mice were habituated to the vivarium for 7 days after arrival. Over the following
9 days the mice were habituated to the experimental personnel and the testing devices.
Baseline allodynia were collected every third day for three consecutive testing times prior to
injection of the anti-collagen antibody cocktail.

3.4 Clinical scores of arthritis
Arthritic clinical scores were assessed at the same time as the allodynia and grip strength
measures were made. The degree of arthritis was assessed by lifting the animal and
inspecting the digits, dorsal aspect of the paws and the ankle joints. Swelling was evaluated
according to the scoring system below. Redness was not considered.
Digits: 0-5 points (1 point for each swollen digit)
Paw: 0 or 5
Ankle: 0 or 5
The maximal score per leg is 15 points. All four legs were scored; hence the maximal score
per mouse was 60 points.
3.5 Tactile Response Threshold
Mice were transferred to a testing cage with a wire mesh bottom and allowed to acclimate
and tactile allodynia was assessed using von Frey filaments and the “up-down method” for
calculation of 50% withdrawal threshold. On days where assessment of allodynia coincided
with antibody treatment, the measurement of allodynia was performed first.
3.5.1 Modified cold allodynia testing (ACETONE SCALE)
Sensitivity to cold is assessed with acetone drop test. One drop of acetone is applied to the
plantar surface of the hind paw and the duration of the response (biting, licking or shaking)
of the paw is measured over 60 seconds after the application. The test is repeated three times
on each paw with a gap of 20 min between the acetone applications and the average is
calculated.
Based on the time spent exhibiting nocifensive behavior the following scores are given:
0: no response,
1: quick withdrawal, flick or stamp of the paw, total reaction time <1s
2: prolonged withdrawal or repeated flicking, total reaction time between 1 to 3s
3: repeated flicking of the paw total reaction time between 3 and 10s; or with
licking of the paw
4: prolonged licking, total reaction time over 10 s.

3.5.2 Thermal allodynia
Heat-evoked paw withdrawal response was assessed using a device similar to that described
by Hargreaves et al., (1988).1 The apparatus consists of a glass surface (maintained at 25°C)
on which the mice are placed individually in Plexiglas cubicles. The thermal nociceptive
stimulus originates from a focused projection bulb positioned below the glass surface. A
timer is actuated by the light source, and latency was defined as the time required for the paw
to show a brisk withdrawal as detected by phosphodiode motion sensors that stopped the
timer and terminated the stimulus. Basal paw withdrawal latencies were assessed on three
separate days prior to injection of CII-antibody cocktail or saline intravenously (day 0) and
the average of these three testing defined as the baseline value. The mice were tested again
day 17 and day 41 and the data expressed as % change in paw withdrawal baseline (PWL)
from baseline (mean ± SEM, n = 6).
3.5.3 Immunohistochemistry
Tissue sections (DRG, 12μm) were incubated with primary antibodies against galanin (rabbit,
1:4000), P2X3 (rabbit, 1:2000), CACNA2D1 (α2δ1, rabbit, 1:1000), GAP43 (rabbit, 1:1000)
at 4°C overnight. Immunoreactivity (IR) was visualized with TSA Plus kit (Perkin Elmer,
Boston, MA). Briefly, the slides were rinsed in TNT buffer (0.1M Tris–HCl, pH 7.4; 0.15 M
NaCl; 0.05% Tween 20) for 15 min, blocked with TNB buffer [0.5% blocking reagent
(Perkin Elmer) in TNT buffer] for 30 min followed by 30 min incubation with horseradish
peroxidase-labeled secondary antibody (1:200, DAKO, Glostrup, Denmark) diluted in TNB
buffer. After 15 min wash in TNT buffer, all sections were exposed to fluorescein diluted in
amplification solution (1:100) for 15 min, and washed in TNT buffer for 20 min. The
sections were counterstained with 0.001% propidium iodide (PI, Sigma) and mounted with
2.5% DABCO (Sigma).
3.5.4 Image Analysis and Quantification
Representative pictures were captured in a LSM710 confocal laser-scanning microscope
(Carl Zeiss, Jena, Germany) operated with ZEN2012 software (Zeiss). Emission spectra for
each dye were limited as follows: Fluorescein (505–540 nm) and PI (590-680 nm). Multi-
panel figures were assembled in Adobe Photoshop CS6 software (Adobe Systems Inc., San
Jose, CA). The proportion (number of immuno-positive neurons / neurons labeled by PI) of
galanin, CACNA2D1, P2X3 and GAP43 positive neurons in DRGs was counted from three
or four randomly selected sections at different levels (with minimum 50 μm in between). The
relative IR intensity between CAIA and control DRGs in the same section was measured by
Image J (NIH), while neurons with intensity more than the mean plus 2 fold of the SD of
background were considered as positive.

4 RESULTS
Anti-collagen antibody/LPS treatment induced a transient increase in arthritis score and a
mechanical hypersensitivity which persisted after inflammation had completely resolved on
day 39.
Pretreatment of murine (B3) and humanized (LT3114) anti-LPA antibodies (from Day -8 to
Day 15) prevented CAIA induced mechanical (Figure 1A) and cold (Figure 1C)
hypersensitivity at day 14, but had no effect on the arthritis clinical score (Figure 1B).
Therapeutic dosing with 10 mg/Kg B3 antibody (from Day 12 to Day 47) reversed
CAIA-induced mechanical hypersensitivity beyond day 39 (time at which inflammation has
completely resolved) (Figure 2B) with no effect on the degree of joint inflammation
(Figure 2A). Treatment with control IgG (LT1015) or 40 mg/kg B3 had no impact on
allodynia. (Figure 2B). Treatment with B3 antibody also reversed heat hypersensitivity in
arthritic mice in both the inflammatory and the post-inflammatory phase (Figure 3A), but the
antibody only attenuated CAIA-induced cold allodynia during the inflammatory phase at day
19, and not at day 44 during the post-inflammatory phase (Figure 3B).
Neuropathic pain is accompanied by changes in the expression of neuronal ion channels,
neurotransmitters, peptides and their receptors. To further understand the actions of anti-LPA
antibodies in the amelioration of CAIA-induced pain, changes in the phenotypic pain profile
of CAIA and antibody treated mice were investigated. Expression levels of the α2δ1 sub-unit
of the high voltage-activated calcium channel, the purinergic 2X ionotropic receptor (P2X3),
the neuropeptide galanin and the growth-associated protein 43 (GAP-43) were assessed by
immunostaining. Also, it is believed that glia mediated central sensitization in the spinal
dorsal horn is a mechanism for pain hypersensitivity, CAIA induced arthritis drives glia
activation characterized by astrocytes (GFAP) and microglia glia (Iba-1) up regulation.
Immunostaining of dorsal root ganglia (DRG) and spinal cords of CAIA mice treated in
therapeutic mode from day 12 to 47 showed that systemic administration of B3 reduced
CAIA-induced up-regulation of α2δ1, P2X3 and galanin in DRGs (Figures 4-6), but had no
effect on GAP43 up-regulation (Figure 7). B3 also prevents CAIA-induced spinal astrocyte
activation (Figure 8), but does not reverse microglial activation in spinal cords from CAIA
mice (Figure 9).

5 CONCLUSIONS
Pre-treatment at 10 mg/kg with the murine (B3) and humanized (LT3114) anti-LPA
antibodies had no effect on arthritis score. However B3 and LT3114 prevented
CAIA-induced mechanical and cold hypersensitivity
Delayed therapy of B3 antibody at 10 mg/kg decreased mechanical and thermal (heat)
hypersensitivity in this model of chronic arthritis pain, but in contrast 40 mg/kg dose of B3
had no effect. Interestingly B3 also improved cold hypersensitivity but only during the early
inflammatory phase of the study.
Systemic treatment of B3 antibody at 10 mg/kg was also effective at reversing CAIA-
induced up-regulation of proteins of stress/nerve-injury markers
