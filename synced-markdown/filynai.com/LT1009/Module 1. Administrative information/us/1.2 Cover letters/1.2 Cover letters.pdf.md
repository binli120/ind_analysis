INTRODUCTION 

 
ASONEP™  [parenteral  sonepcizumab  (LT1009)]  is  a  recombinant,  humanized,  IgG1κ,  biospecific 
monoclonal  antibody  that  binds  sphingosine  1-phosphate  (S1P),  a  pro-angiogenic  and  pro-growth 
bioactive lipid that is structurally identical in humans and all species studied.  
 

 

Sphingosine 1-phosphate (S1P) 

 

 
LT1009 demonstrates anti-angiogenic and anti-growth effects in murine models of human cancer.   Lpath 
is developing ASONEP™ [parenteral sonepcizumab, LT1009] for the following therapeutic indication: 
 

ASONEP™ [parenteral sonepcizumab (LT1009)] is indicated for use as a single agent 
and in combination with TBD cytotoxic and other anti-angiogenic agents as second-line 
therapy  to  treat  patients  with  unresectable,  locally  advanced,  recurrent  or  metastatic 
TBD cancers.  
 

LT1002,  a  murine  anti-S1P  antibody,  was  produced  by  Lpath  in  mice  following  immunization  with  a 
proprietary protein-S1P immunogen. A polyclonal antibody with favorable properties was identified and 
used  to  produce  a  monoclonal  antibody  using  standard  hybridoma  culture  techniques.  LT1002  was 
humanized  after  extensive  efficacy  and  safety  evaluations  in  mice  by  inserting  murine  CDRs  into  a 
human  antibody  framework  to  produce  LT1009.  This  effort  was  guided  by  sequence  identity  and 
homology searches as well as computer modeling. The humanized antibody was further optimized with 
respect to binding and stability by a total of 6 selective amino acid substitutions (1 in the CDR region and 
5 in the antibody framework region). The resulting antibody, LT1009, had a similar binding affinity as 
LT1002, <0.1 nM, but better thermal stability [thermal unfolding transitions (Tm) of LT1002 was 55±2°C 
and the Tm of LT1009 was 73±2°C. 
 
If  the  FDA  approves  this  submission,  Lpath  intends  to  initiate  clinical  development  of  LT1009  for 
oncology indications in refractory solid tumors.  A battery of safety studies has been completed with both 
LT1009  and  LT1002  to  support  dosing  in  humans.  Sufficient  amounts  of  LT1009,  produced  in  cell 
culture  using  CHO  cells  transfected  with  genes  to  produce  the  antibody,  are  available  for  initiation  of 
development activities.  
 
What  follows  is  some  background  information  on  ASONEP™  [parenteral  sonepcizumab,  LT1009]  to 
support  the  evaluation  of  this  IND.  A  list  of  development  activities  for  ASONEP™  compared  to 
FDA/ICH Guidelines is provided in Appendix I.

tumor-associated angiogenesis and like ceramide, makes cancer cells more susceptible to apoptotic cell

of the kinase have been identified, SPHK1 and SPHK2; both of which are recognized as oncogenes8, 9.

SAFETY AND DISPOSITION 

A battery of preclinical studies with sonepcizumab/LT1009 complying with the following applicable ICH 
guidelines  was  performed  to  support  the  initial  clinical  development  of  the  antibody  in  the  proposed 
clinical indication: 
•  Toxicokinetics  and  Pharmacokinetics  (ICH  S3  A/B):  Studies  were  performed  to  determine  the 
pharmacokinetic  profile  of  sonepcizumab/LT1009  in  monkeys,  rabbits  and  with  LT1002  (murine 
variant of sonepcizumab/LT1009) in mice. Using validated bioanalytical methods, systemic exposure 
and toxicokinetic profiles were determined in repeat-dose toxicology studies in monkeys and mice. 
Plasma concentrations in these studies were shown to be approximately proportional to dose and no 
gender effects on antibody clearance were apparent. 

 
•  General  Toxicity  (ICH  S4  A/B):  Pivotal  toxicology  studies  were  performed  in  male  and  female 
monkeys and mice using qd and q3d dosing, respectively. The ranges of doses in these studies were 
sufficient to elicit evidence of a slight decrease in plasma lymphocyte counts in the monkeys at the 
highest dose (100 mg/kg, q3d) and more marked lymphopenia in mice (all dose levels, qd). In these 
species, the lymphopenia appears related to the magnitude of the dose.  

C57BL6 mice were generally tolerant to large, single doses of LT1002 with no evidence of toxicity 
up to 240 mg/kg administered iv. Administration of drug to mice over 28 days produced some signs 
of hematological toxicity (decreased lymphocytes) at daily doses ≥ 25 mg/kg. Cynomolgus monkeys 
proved to be somewhat less sensitive to sonepcizumab/LT1009. Even at 30 mg/kg (cumulative dose 
over 28 days of 840 mg/kg), the lymphopenia observed in mice was more pronounced with LT1002 
than the very  mild, reversible lymphopenia induced in  monkeys with a similar 100 mg/kg dose of 
sonepcizumab/LT1009 (cumulative dose of 1000 mg/kg).  

Besides  lymphopenia,  no  other  consistent  adverse  event  was  noted  in  mice  treated  with  LT1002. 
Slight  decreases  in  plasma  lymphocyte  levels  were  observed  in  NHPs  treated  with  100  mg/kg 
sonepcizumab/LT1009  but  these  values  returned  to  normal  during  the  recovery  period.  For  both 
sonepcizumab/LT1009 and LT1002, no effects were seen in animal mortality, clinical observations, 
body weight, clinical pathology (hematology except for lymphocytes, clinical chemistry, coagulation, 
and  urinalysis)  and  lymphocyte  cell  phenotyping.  For  monkeys,  no  change  was  also  observed  for 
ophthalmology  and  cardiovascular  function  by  ECG.  Mild  histopathologic  changes  were  noted  in 
mice only in the spleen. No histopathologic changes were noted in monkeys dosed with LT1009. In 
Cynomolgus monkeys, anti-sonepcizumab/LT1009 antibodies were detected after 28 days of dosing.  
This immunogenic response was dose-dependent.

ADME 

The pharmacokinetic and/or toxicokinetic profile of sonepcizumab was determined in Cynomolgus 
monkeys, C57/BL6 mice and New Zealand White rabbits.  
 

•  Sonepcizumab  exhibited  approximate  dose  proportional  increases  in  systemic  exposure  in 

Cynomolgus monkeys following iv administration.  

•  The plasma half-lives of LT1009 in mice, monkeys and rabbits were approximately 5, 2 and 2 

days, respectively.  

•  The mean clearance value in the NHPs and rabbits were similar, approximately 2 mL/h/kg but 

was approximately 3-fold slower in mice.  

•  The volume of distribution at steady state in all three species was approximately the same, 100 

mL/kg. This value is similar to the expected vascular volume of any of the three species.  

•  No gender differences were apparent between pharmacokinetic parameters estimates determined 

from data from male and female animals.

SUMMARY 

The  anti-S1P  approach  to  treat  cancer  has  unique  potential  to  affect  the  growth  of  tumor  cells  while 
simultaneously  inhibiting  tumor-associated  angiogenesis  by  directly  inhibiting  endothelial  cells  and 
indirectly reducing pro-angiogenic cytokines such as VEGF, FGF and the interleukins. Data suggest that 
neutralizing  S1P  with  LT1009  may  block  the  progression  of  multiple  tumor  lineages.  Thus,  LT1009 
represents a novel, non-cytotoxic and specific therapy that potentially will provide an effective therapy 
for cancer. 

Targeting  a  ligand  for  the  purpose  of  intervening  in  a  signaling  cascade  is  an  effective  therapeutic 
approach. Monoclonal antibodies including Avastin®, Remicade® and Humira® are clinically used as 
“molecular sponges” to selectively absorb and neutralize the ligands that would otherwise activate their 
receptors. Lpath believes that “sponging” of S1P with LT1009 will also provide clinical benefit like these 
marketed antibodies. 
 
The initial Phase 1 clinical study for LT1009 will be a multi-center, open-label, Phase 1 study to evaluate 
the safety and tolerability of LT1009 as a single agent in subjects with refractory advanced solid tumors. 
Evidence of efficacy will also be sought in this study. Subjects will be treated with multiple weekly doses 
of LT1009 by parenteral administration over 90 minutes. Primary and secondary endpoints of the study 
will  be  assessed  at  various  times  during  the  study  and  after  the  last  dose.  Safety  will  be  evaluated  by 
assessing adverse events (including study treatment induced toxicities), vital signs, physical examination 
findings,  immunogenicity  and  clinical  laboratory  parameters.  Secondary  endpoints  are  defining  the 
pharmacokinetic,  immunogenicity  and  S1P  binding  performance  profiles  of  LT1009  administered  to 
cancer patients with solid tumors.

The molecule consists of two heavy chains and two light chains that are linked by disulfide bridges. The 
heavy  chain  has  a  theoretical  MW  of  49,628.4  daltons  (experimentally  determined  by  MS:  49,621 
daltons) and the light chain has a MW of 23,525.2 daltons (experimentally determined by MS: 23,521 
daltons). All 32 cysteine residues of the heavy and light chains are used to form disulfide bridges of the 
quadromeric  molecule;  11  in  the  heavy  chain  and  5  in  the  light  chain.  The  heavy  chains  are  87% 
glycosylated at Asparagine301. The most prominent glycoform is (N-acetylhexosamine)2 (Deoxyhexose)1 
+ (Mannose)3 (N-acetylglucosamine)2. The molecule contains no sialic acid. 
 
The average isoelectric point (PI) of the antibody is 7.4. There are three isoforms with PI’s at 7.5, 7.4 and 
7.35.  The  PI  of  the  light  chain  is  5.6.  The  PI’s  of  the  heavy  chain  are  between  8.5  and  9.5.  Peptide 
mapping revealed that Asparagine388 is partially deamidated to aspartate. This likely explains the three 
major PI isoforms of the intact molecule. 
 
The intact antibody exhibits high affinity to its target molecule sphingosine 1-phosphate (S1P).  The KD is 
51 nM. 
 
Cell  line  development  was  performed  at  Laureate  Pharma,  Princeton,  NJ.  Only  chemically-defined, 
protein-free  CD-CHO  medium  was  used  during  cell  expansion,  transfections,  cell  line  generation, 
subcloning and master cell bank generation. CHO-K1SV host cells were transfected with the linearized 
pATH1009 vector. Transfected clones were selected based on growth and productivity. Clone CHO-LH1-
275  was  selected  for  master  cell  banking  and  ultimate  use  in  GMP-conforming  production.  Further 
cloning  of  CHO-LH1-275  demonstrated  that  all  its  descendants  were  comparable  in  their  growth 
characteristics, their cell specific productivity and the number of gene copies for both, heavy and light 
chains suggesting true clonality of the parent clone. The CHO-LH1-275 Master Cell Bank (MCB) was 
created  at  BioReliance,  Rockville,  MD,  under  GMP  compliance.  The  MCB  is  stored  under  GMP 
compliance in the vapor phase of liquid nitrogen in individual cryovials at Laureate Pharma (Princeton, 
NJ) for the generation of production batches and at BioReliance, Rockville, MD, for backup. The MCB 
was shown to be free of known adventitious agents.  
 
CHO-LH1-275  cells  are  proliferated  employing  protein-free  nutrient  that  is  free  from  adventitious 
contaminants  in  conventional  cell  culture  equipment  including  conventional  suspension  bioreactors.  At 
the end of the bioreactor run, the product containing nutrient is clarified by filtration and subsequently 
purified by conventional purification processes involving Protein-A product binding, QAE contaminant 
binding and nanofiltration to remove potentially present, but yet unknown, viruses. The purified antibody 
is concentrated by ultrafiltration to the desired concentration before the buffer is changed to the final drug 
product  formulation  buffer  by  diafiltration.  Post-diafiltration,  polysorbate  80  is  added  to  a  final 
concentration of 0.02% to minimize the formation of product aggregates. This final formulation is filtered 
through a 0.2-µm membrane and stored at 2 to 8ºC. 
 
Sonepcizumab is formulated into the drug product ASONEP™. The current formulation of ASONEP™ is 
10 mg sonepcizumab per mL of 24 mM sodium phosphate, 148 mM sodium chloride, 0.02% polysorbate 
80, pH 6.5.  This formulation is supplied as a sterile 5 mL solution in 10 mL serum vials that are sealed 
with a conventional PTFE-coated elastomeric stopper that is secured in place by a crimped-on aluminum 
overseal. 
 
Depending on the individual patient dose, the content of one or more ASONEP™ vials is injected into a 
standard 0.9% saline iv bag at the test site. The diluted antibody is then intravenously administered to 
cancer patients within eight hours of preparation.
